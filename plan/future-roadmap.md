# Future Physical Capability Roadmap

## Purpose

This document is a **future roadmap**, not a change to the current workout plan.

The current v3 plan remains the priority while consistency is rebuilt, the cut continues, Just Run / Zero-to-5K progresses, strength returns, and calisthenics foundations improve.

This roadmap captures physical qualities and skills that may be worth developing later so they are not forgotten or added impulsively to the current program.

## Current priorities

1. Maintain a sustainable cut while retaining/regaining muscle.
2. Rebuild consistent strength training.
3. Complete the current Zero-to-5K progression.
4. Build strong calisthenics fundamentals.
5. Maintain and gradually improve general mobility.
6. Preserve recovery and consistency rather than trying to maximize every quality at once.

# Long-term physical capability categories

## 1. Muscle / hypertrophy

Goal: maintain or build useful muscle mass and physique.

Current coverage: strong.

Possible measurements:
- bodyweight trend;
- waist measurement;
- progress photos;
- training volume/load trends.

## 2. Strength

Goal: produce high force with good technique through useful ranges of motion.

Current coverage: strong.

Possible measurements:
- standardized top sets at a known RIR;
- load/reps on squat or leg press, RDL, pressing, rows;
- pull-up and dip strength;
- eventual estimated 1RM where useful.

## 3. Strength endurance / work capacity

Goal: repeat submaximal force without rapid performance loss.

Current coverage: moderate to strong through calisthenics and general training.

Possible measurements:
- repeat-set performance;
- clean push-up / pull-up / dip volume;
- time or reps in simple standardized circuits if this becomes a goal.

## 4. Aerobic endurance

Goal: sustain moderate effort efficiently for long periods.

Current coverage: strong through Zero-to-5K.

Possible future measurements:
- 5K time;
- easy-run pace at a standardized RPE;
- duration of comfortable continuous running;
- optional resting heart-rate trend.

## 5. Anaerobic conditioning

Goal: perform and recover from repeated harder efforts.

Current coverage: developing; do not stack extra hard conditioning on top of Zero-to-5K right now.

Possible future work:
- intervals;
- hill repeats;
- short hard efforts with controlled recovery.

## 6. Mobility / flexibility

Goal: achieve useful joint range of motion comfortably.

Current coverage: strong as general mobility practice, but progress is not yet highly quantified.

Future mobility-skill goals of interest:
- front split;
- middle split;
- pancake;
- bridge / backbend;
- comfortable deep squat;
- full controlled Cossack;
- improved shoulder flexion and extension.

Possible measurements:
- knee-to-wall ankle distance;
- split distance from the floor;
- pancake torso-to-floor distance;
- standardized shoulder-flexion test;
- bridge position / shoulder opening;
- deep-squat comfort and control.

## 7. Active mobility / control through range

Goal: not only reach a position, but actively control it with muscular strength.

Examples:
- Cossack squat;
- controlled deep squat;
- pike and pancake compression;
- straight-leg raises;
- L-sit / V-sit progression;
- controlled bridge work;
- German hang / skin-the-cat progression;
- press-to-handstand preparation.

## 8. Calisthenics / body control

Goal: control bodyweight through progressively more demanding positions and movements.

Current coverage: strong.

Priority tracks already include:
- push-up;
- pull-up;
- dip;
- pike push-up / eventual HSPU;
- handstand;
- L-sit;
- unilateral squat progression.

Possible measurements:
- progression level;
- reps;
- assistance used;
- hold duration;
- ROM quality.

## 9. Core stability and force transfer

Goal: keep the trunk stable while limbs produce or absorb force.

Current coverage: good.

Possible future additions if needed:
- anti-rotation work;
- carries;
- more dynamic trunk control.

## 10. Balance / proprioception

Goal: control body position using visual, vestibular, and proprioceptive information.

Current coverage: moderate through handstands, unilateral squats, single-leg RDLs, and Cossacks, but not yet trained or measured as a dedicated quality.

Future options:
- single-leg stance;
- eyes-closed single-leg stance;
- single-leg reach patterns;
- hop-and-stick landings;
- dynamic unilateral balance;
- handstand progression.

Possible measurements:
- best single-leg stance time per side;
- eyes-closed stance time;
- handstand hold time;
- controlled unilateral squat depth/quality.

## 11. Coordination

Goal: sequence multiple body parts accurately with appropriate timing, rhythm, and force.

Current coverage: low to moderate indirectly through calisthenics and unilateral work.

Future options:
- jump rope;
- skipping;
- cross-body crawling;
- bear-crawl variations;
- simple footwork patterns;
- lateral movement drills;
- throwing/catching while stationary or moving;
- increasingly complex calisthenics transitions.

Possible measurements:
- longest unbroken jump-rope set;
- errors per minute;
- progression through a chosen coordination drill;
- successful controlled repetitions of a movement sequence.

## 12. Power / explosiveness

Goal: produce force quickly.

Current coverage: weak and the clearest major gap in the current plan.

Possible future work:
- pogo jumps;
- vertical jumps;
- standing broad jumps;
- low-volume bounds;
- explosive push-ups;
- medicine-ball throws if equipment is available;
- short sprints later if appropriate.

Possible measurements:
- standing broad-jump distance;
- vertical-jump height;
- explosive push-up variation/reps;
- short sprint time.

Do not add high-volume plyometrics while rebuilding training consistency. Power work should remain low-volume and high-quality.

## 13. Reactive ability / landing skill

Goal: absorb force and respond rapidly without losing position.

Current coverage: minimal.

Future options:
- jump-and-stick landings;
- lateral hop-and-stick;
- low-level reactive hops;
- progressively more dynamic landing drills.

## 14. Speed

Goal: move the body rapidly over short distances.

Current coverage: limited; normal running does not fully train sprint speed.

Future options after running base and tissue tolerance improve:
- strides;
- short accelerations;
- short sprints;
- hill sprints.

## 15. Agility / change of direction

Goal: accelerate, decelerate, and change direction efficiently and under control.

Current coverage: minimal.

Future options:
- lateral shuffle;
- carioca/grapevine;
- simple cone patterns;
- planned cuts;
- reactive change-of-direction drills if this becomes a priority.

## 16. Unilateral strength

Goal: produce and control force one side at a time.

Current coverage: strong.

Examples already present:
- single-leg squat progressions;
- single-leg RDL;
- Cossacks / lateral squats.

## 17. Lateral and rotational movement

Goal: be capable outside the usual forward/backward sagittal plane and control rotation.

Current coverage: partial through Cossacks and unilateral work.

Future options:
- lateral lunges / Cossacks;
- lateral hops;
- controlled rotational patterns;
- anti-rotation and rotational trunk work;
- throwing patterns if desired.

## 18. Grip / hanging / climbing ability

Goal: use the hands, forearms, shoulders, and trunk to hang, support, carry, and climb.

Current coverage: moderate through pull-ups and rings.

Future options:
- dead and active hangs;
- loaded carries;
- varied hanging;
- rope or climbing work;
- bouldering / climbing if desired.

## 19. Locomotion and general movement ability

Goal: move effectively through the environment using a range of basic human movement patterns.

Examples:
- run;
- jump;
- land;
- crawl;
- climb;
- carry;
- throw;
- catch;
- balance;
- change direction.

This does not mean every pattern needs a dedicated weekly workout. It is a long-term capability checklist.

## 20. Resilience / movement tolerance

Goal: tolerate a useful variety of positions, ranges, speeds, and loads without excessive irritation or loss of control.

This should emerge gradually from sensible strength, mobility, running, calisthenics, power, and varied movement practice rather than from random high-risk drills.

# What "movement skills" means

A **movement skill** is a learned physical task that combines multiple capacities such as strength, mobility, balance, coordination, timing, spatial awareness, and technique.

It differs from a simple capacity measurement:

- A stronger squat measures strength.
- A deeper passive stretch measures flexibility.
- A movement skill requires the body to coordinate those capacities to accomplish a task.

Examples of movement skills:
- handstand;
- jump rope;
- jumping and landing accurately;
- climbing;
- crawling patterns;
- throwing and catching;
- skin-the-cat;
- controlled bridge;
- press-to-handstand;
- balancing while moving on one leg;
- linking calisthenics movements smoothly.

Movement skills are not automatically more important than strength or endurance. They are useful because they make physical qualities more usable and transferable.

# Possible future benchmark dashboard

Retest roughly every 6-8 weeks when these qualities become active priorities.

- **Strength:** standardized squat/leg-press and RDL performance; pull-ups/dips.
- **Cardio:** 5K time or standardized easy-run pace/RPE.
- **Power:** standing broad jump or vertical jump.
- **Calisthenics:** pull-up reps, handstand hold, L-sit hold, progression levels.
- **Balance:** single-leg stance and/or eyes-closed stance.
- **Coordination:** jump-rope unbroken reps or errors/minute.
- **Ankle mobility:** knee-to-wall distance.
- **Hip/hamstring mobility:** pike/pancake or split measurements.
- **Shoulder mobility:** standardized overhead-flexion measurement.
- **Backbend:** bridge position / shoulder-opening benchmark.

The dashboard should remain small enough that testing does not become its own training program.

# Likely future sequence

## Phase 0 — Current
Keep v3 essentially unchanged. Build consistency, strength, running capacity, calisthenics fundamentals, and general mobility.

## Phase 1 — Add measurement
Once training is stable, establish a small capability benchmark set. Do not add large amounts of new training yet.

## Phase 2 — Add low-volume power
Introduce a small power primer, likely once per week initially, while monitoring running and lower-body recovery.

## Phase 3 — Formalize mobility skills
Replace some generic mobility volume with goal-specific progressions such as front split + bridge, then rotate goals over time.

## Phase 4 — Add balance / coordination / athletic movement
Use small 5-10 minute blocks or substitutions rather than simply making workouts longer. Possible tools include jump rope, landing practice, crawling, lateral movement, and simple balance drills.

## Phase 5 — Rebuild cardio structure after Zero-to-5K
Reassess whether the long-term running setup should include an easy aerobic run, a faster quality session, and a longer/easy run.

## Phase 6 — Specialize selectively
Choose future blocks according to interest: advanced calisthenics, splits/backbends, climbing, sprinting, agility, power, or other movement skills. Not every capacity must be maximized at once.

---

# Guiding principle

The long-term goal is not to collect as many exercises as possible. It is to become broadly capable while keeping training sustainable:

**strong + muscular + conditioned + mobile + powerful + balanced + coordinated + skilled at moving the body.**

New work should usually **replace, rotate, or specialize existing work**, not endlessly add volume to the week.
