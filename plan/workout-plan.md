# Workout Plan (v3 - hybrid strength + calisthenics + running + mobility)

**Primary goal:** cut from roughly 175 lb toward ~151 lb while building/retaining muscle and strength, developing a strong calisthenics foundation for advanced skills later, improving running stamina, and maintaining or improving mobility/flexibility.

This plan uses **two hybrid gym/home strength days, one protected pure-calisthenics day, three Just Run sessions, and one full rest day**.

Gym loading is useful but **not required for plan continuity**. Every gym-dependent movement has a home fallback so a missed gym window does not become a missed workout.

## Core rules

- **Supersets are preferred for time efficiency, not mandatory.** Pair exercises when it does not meaningfully reduce performance or technique.
- A superset means: A1 -> ~15-30 sec transition -> A2 -> prescribed rest. It does **not** mean zero rest or turning strength work into cardio.
- Heavy or performance-sensitive work such as squats, RDLs, and eventually heavy weighted pull-ups may use straight sets and longer rest.
- **Normal strength/hypertrophy work:** finish most sets with about **1-2 reps in reserve (RIR)**.
- **Skill work:** stop before technique breaks down. Most skill sets should feel easier than hard hypertrophy sets.
- **Restart phase:** use **2-3 RIR** and/or remove one working set/round from major exercises for the first 1-2 weeks back.
- Use controlled range of motion and gradually earn harder/deeper positions.
- Keep at least **1 full rest day** each week.
- Do not double sessions to make up missed work; slide the schedule forward or use the home fallback.

## Weekly schedule while using Just Run: Zero to 5K

- **Day 1 — Upper Hybrid Strength + Calisthenics Fundamentals + Mobility** (~55-65 min)
- **Day 2 — Just Run + Lower Mobility** (~40-50 min)
- **Day 3 — Lower Hybrid Strength + Unilateral Skill + Mobility** (~55-65 min)
- **Day 4 — Just Run + Full-Body Mobility** (~40-50 min)
- **Day 5 — Pure Calisthenics Skill + Strength + Mobility** (~50-65 min)
- **Day 6 — Just Run + Mobility** (~40-50 min)
- **Day 7 — Full rest**

Calendar days can shift. Preserve the order and recovery rather than forcing missed sessions into the same calendar week.

### Restart ramp

For the first 1-2 weeks back:
- use **2-3 RIR**;
- complete **4-5 sessions** if 6 is too much initially;
- reduce one working set from larger movements when needed;
- do not restart the whole plan or make up missed sessions with doubles;
- build toward the full schedule as soreness, sleep, and consistency improve.

# Day 1 — Upper Hybrid Strength + Calisthenics Fundamentals

Purpose: build upper-body muscle and general strength while continuing the pull-up, dip, and push-up foundations needed for advanced calisthenics later.

## Warm-up Superset A — 2 rounds
- **A1. Scapular pull-ups** — 6-10 reps
- **A2. Scapular push-ups** — 8-12 reps
- Rest **30-45 sec**

## Warm-up Superset B — 2 rounds
- **B1. Band shoulder pass-throughs/openers** — 10 controlled reps
- **B2. Thoracic rotations** — 6-8/side
- Rest **30-45 sec**

## Strength Superset A — 3 rounds
- **A1. Pull-up progression** — 4-8 reps
- **A2. Dumbbell or machine chest press** — 6-10 reps
- Rest **90-120 sec**

If either movement clearly reduces performance on the other, use straight sets instead.

## Strength Superset B — 3 rounds
- **B1. Cable row or chest-supported row** — 8-12 reps
- **B2. Dip progression** — 6-10 reps
- Rest **90 sec**

## Foundation Superset C — 2 rounds
- **C1. Push-up progression** — 8-15 reps
- **C2. Hollow hold** — 20-40 sec
- Rest **60-90 sec**

Keep these push-up sets clean and usually around **2 RIR**. Day 5 is the higher-priority calisthenics exposure.

## Accessory/Mobility Superset D — 2 rounds
- **D1. Lateral raise** — 10-20 reps
- **D2. Wall slides** — 10 reps
- Rest **45-60 sec**

## Mobility Superset E — 1-2 rounds
- **E1. Thread-the-needle** — 8/side
- **E2. Shoulder CARs** — 5/side

### Day 1 home fallback

If the gym is inconvenient, do the session at home instead of skipping it:

- Chest press -> **weighted-vest/backpack push-up, deep ring push-up, or current harder push-up variation**
- Cable/chest-supported row -> **ring row**
- Lateral raise -> **band lateral raise**
- Pull-up, dip, push-up, core, and mobility work -> **unchanged**

The home version is a complete training day, not a punishment or make-up session.

# Calisthenics progression tracks

## Progression priority tiers

### Tier 1 — actively track and progress
- Push-up progression
- Pull-up progression
- Dip progression
- Pike push-up / eventual HSPU progression
- Handstand progression
- L-sit progression
- Primary single-leg squat progression (pistol/shrimp branch)

These are the main calisthenics performance markers.

### Tier 2 — useful movement qualities, not mandatory advanced skills
- Cossack/lateral squat
- Single-leg RDL/balance
- 90/90 hip rotation
- ankle mobility
- deep squat comfort
- wrist mobility
- shoulder/thoracic mobility

Improve them, but do not chase increasingly exotic variations unless they become personal goals.

## Push-up track — foundation first, advanced pushing later

1. Incline push-up (if needed)
2. Floor push-up
3. Feet-elevated push-up or ring push-up
4. Deep ring push-up
5. Weighted-vest/backpack push-up
6. Pseudo-planche push-up / planche-lean preparation when the foundation is strong
7. Optional later specialization: dedicated planche progression

**Current philosophy:** do not rush planche-specific training. First build strong push-ups, dips, pike pushing, scapular control, core control, and wrist tolerance. Planche can become a specialization block later.

## Pull-up track — foundation first, advanced pulling later

1. Band-assisted pull-up / controlled negatives
2. Full strict pull-up
3. Stronger strict sets across the rep range
4. Tempo or chest-to-bar pull-up
5. Weighted pull-up
6. Optional later branches:
   - explosive/high pull-up -> muscle-up work
   - archer/uneven pulling -> assisted one-arm progression
   - L-sit, pause, tempo, or other control variations

There is no need to choose the final advanced branch yet. Build strong strict and weighted pulling first.

Default grip: **neutral grip when available**, otherwise a comfortable roughly shoulder-width overhand grip.

## Dip track
1. Ring/bar support hold
2. Feet-assisted or band-assisted dip
3. Full dip
4. Deeper controlled dip as tolerated
5. Weighted dip or more demanding ring variation

## Pike/HSPU track
1. Pike hold / basic pike push-up
2. Strong floor pike push-up
3. Feet-elevated pike push-up
4. Wall-supported HSPU partial range
5. Deeper/full wall HSPU progression
6. Advanced freestanding work later if desired

## Handstand track
1. Wall-supported hold
2. Chest-to-wall hold with improved line
3. Weight shifts / shoulder taps
4. Controlled kick-up practice
5. Freestanding hold progression

## L-sit track
1. Support hold / knee tuck
2. Tuck L-sit
3. One-leg L-sit
4. Full L-sit
5. Longer holds / harder compression variations

## Primary lower-body skill track — unilateral squat strength

1. Supported single-leg sit-to-stand or high box single-leg squat
2. Controlled step-down / skater squat to a target
3. Lower box single-leg squat, assisted pistol, or assisted shrimp squat
4. Full pistol **or** full shrimp squat, depending on preference and anatomy
5. Weighted, deficit, slower-tempo, or other advanced unilateral variations

The endpoint does not have to be a pistol squat; choose the branch that fits anatomy, mobility, and preference.

## Cossack/lateral squat track — mobility + lateral strength

1. Supported lateral squat / shallow Cossack
2. Controlled full Cossack squat
3. Optional lightly loaded/deeper Cossack if useful

Stop here unless advanced rotational lower-body skills become a deliberate goal. Cossacks mainly provide lateral movement, adductor strength/flexibility, hip mobility, ankle mobility, and control in a deep position.

## Hinge/balance track
1. Supported bodyweight single-leg RDL
2. Unsupported single-leg RDL
3. Slow-tempo / longer-reach single-leg RDL
4. Loaded backpack/vest single-leg RDL if desired

### Graduation rule
Progress a strength movement when you can complete the prescribed sets at the top of the current range with:
- clean technique and controlled range;
- roughly **1-2 RIR**;
- no sharp pain or joint pinching;
- stable performance across at least **2 sessions**.

For skills, progress when technique is consistently clean and the current step is controlled. Do not force a harder named skill simply because it is next on a list.

# Gym strength progression

Track these as simple load/repetition performance markers:

- chest press
- cable/chest-supported row
- squat **or** leg press
- Romanian deadlift

Use double progression:
1. Keep the load stable while building reps within the prescribed range.
2. When all working sets reach the top of the range with ~1-2 RIR and clean technique, increase the load by the smallest practical increment.
3. Return toward the lower end of the rep range and build again.

Accessories such as lateral raises, leg curls, and calf raises follow the same basic idea but do not need aggressive load jumps.

# Mobility/flexibility coverage

The plan deliberately preserves the mobility coverage from v2:

- **Ankles:** knee-to-wall, ankle rocks, calf stretching
- **Hip flexors:** couch/hip-flexor stretch
- **Hip rotation:** 90/90 work
- **Adductors/deep/lateral positions:** frog stretch, Cossack practice, deep squat holds
- **Hamstrings:** RDLs, leg curls/sliders, hamstring floss
- **Thoracic spine:** rotations, thread-the-needle
- **Shoulders/scapulae:** wall slides, CARs, band openers, scapular work
- **Wrists:** wrist prep and stretching for handstand and future advanced pushing

### Minimum mobility rule
- Normal training day: **~8-15 min** of mobility is built in.
- Busy/low-energy day: do a **2-5 min minimum** rather than making mobility all-or-nothing.
- If desired, add one **25-40 min easy mobility/yoga session** on a recovery day, but not at the expense of the full rest day when recovery is poor.

# Progression and recovery rules

- Normal strength/hypertrophy work: **1-2 RIR** on most working sets.
- Skill work: stop before technique deterioration; do not chase failure.
- Restart weeks: **2-3 RIR** and/or remove one working set from larger exercises.
- Progress with one variable at a time: reps, load, assistance, range of motion, leverage, or hold time.
- Training to momentary failure is not required.
- Deload after roughly **4-6 hard weeks or sooner if recovery clearly deteriorates**: reduce total hard working sets by about 30-40% for around one week.
- After 2+ nights of poor sleep or unusual soreness, reduce volume about 20-30% while keeping technique clean.
- Persistent or sharp joint pain is a reason to stop/regress the movement, not push through it.

### Concurrent running rule
Three beginner Just Run sessions are compatible with the resistance plan, but recovery still matters. Do not add extra hard running while building the base. If lower-body strength, run quality, or soreness consistently worsens, reduce training stress before adding more work.

### Cutting/recovery rule
The plan is being performed during a calorie deficit. The immediate target is to **retain/regain muscle, build strength, and improve skills while losing weight gradually**. If bodyweight is falling quickly while strength, running performance, sleep, soreness, or hunger deteriorate for multiple weeks, review recovery and calorie intake before assuming the workout needs more volume.

# Weekly self-audit

The plan is working if most of these are true:

- bodyweight trend is moving appropriately for the cut;
- strength is stable or improving overall;
- push-up, pull-up, dip, pike/HSPU, handstand, L-sit, and unilateral-squat foundations are stable or improving;
- Just Run sessions are progressing without excessive leg/joint soreness;
- mobility benchmarks are stable or improving;
- no persistent joint pain;
- recovery is good enough to complete most of the schedule;
- gym timing problems are solved with home fallbacks instead of skipped sessions.

If several of these deteriorate together for 2+ weeks, review sleep, calories, total hard-set volume, and running fatigue before adding exercises.
