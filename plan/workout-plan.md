# Workout Plan (v2 - cut + running + full-body calisthenics + mobility)

**Primary goal:** cut from roughly 175 lb toward ~151 lb while retaining/regaining muscle and improving running stamina, calisthenics skills, mobility, and flexibility.

Built for **home + outdoors** with a pull-up bar, rings, resistance bands, weighted vest/backpack, mat, blocks, and foam roller.

## Core rules

- **All non-running work is paired as supersets.** A superset means: A1 -> ~15-30 sec transition -> A2 -> prescribed rest. It does **not** mean zero rest or turning strength work into cardio.
- **Normal strength work:** finish most sets with **1-2 reps in reserve (RIR)**.
- **Restart phase:** use **2-3 RIR** and reduce a main pair by ~1 round when needed for the first 1-2 weeks.
- **Skills:** stop before technique breaks down. Skill work is practice, not failure training.
- Use controlled, comfortable range of motion and gradually earn deeper/harder positions.
- Keep at least **1 full rest day** each week. Do not double sessions to make up missed work; slide the schedule forward instead.

## Weekly schedule while using Just Run: Zero to 5K

Just Run's Zero-to-5K program uses **3 workouts per week**, so it replaces the old separate interval/tempo prescriptions.

- **Day 1 — Upper Strength + Core + Upper Mobility** (~50-60 min)
- **Day 2 — Just Run workout + Lower Mobility** (~40-50 min)
- **Day 3 — Lower Strength + Lower-Body Skill + Core + Mobility** (~55-65 min)
- **Day 4 — Just Run workout + Full-Body Mobility** (~40-50 min)
- **Day 5 — Full-Body Calisthenics Skill + Strength + Mobility** (~50-60 min)
- **Day 6 — Just Run workout + Mobility** (~40-50 min)
- **Day 7 — Full rest**

Calendar days can shift. Preserve the order and recovery rather than forcing missed sessions into the same week.

### Restart ramp

For the first 1-2 weeks back:
- use **2-3 RIR**;
- completing **4-5 sessions** is acceptable if 6 is too much initially;
- do not restart the whole plan or make up missed sessions with doubles;
- build toward the full 3 strength/skill + 3 Just Run structure as soreness, sleep, and consistency improve.

# Calisthenics progression tracks

The plan should not rely on skills appearing automatically. Strength work builds prerequisites, but the desired skills are practiced explicitly.

## Push-up track
A typical path:
1. Incline push-up (if needed)
2. Floor push-up
3. Feet-elevated push-up or ring push-up
4. Deep ring push-up
5. Weighted-vest/backpack push-up
6. Optional branches: archer push-up, pseudo-planche push-up, other advanced variations

## Pull-up track
A typical path:
1. Band-assisted pull-up / controlled negatives
2. Full pull-up
3. Full pull-up for stronger sets across the rep range
4. Tempo or chest-to-bar pull-up
5. Weighted pull-up
6. Optional branch: archer-type pulling progressions

Default grip: **neutral grip when available**, otherwise a comfortable roughly shoulder-width overhand grip.

## Dip track
1. Ring/bar support hold
2. Feet-assisted or band-assisted dip
3. Full dip
4. Deeper controlled dip as tolerated
5. Weighted dip or more demanding ring variation

## Handstand track
1. Wall-supported hold
2. Chest-to-wall hold with improved line
3. Weight shifts / shoulder taps
4. Controlled kick-up practice
5. Freestanding hold progression

## L-sit track
1. Support hold / knee tuck
2. Tuck L-sit
3. One-leg L-sit
4. Full L-sit
5. Longer holds / harder compression variations

## Primary lower-body skill track — unilateral squat strength
This track is intentionally broad; the endpoint does **not** have to be a pistol squat.

1. Supported single-leg sit-to-stand or high box single-leg squat
2. Controlled step-down / skater squat to a target
3. Lower box single-leg squat, assisted pistol, or assisted shrimp squat
4. Full pistol **or** full shrimp squat, depending on preference and anatomy
5. Weighted, deficit, slower-tempo, or other advanced unilateral variations

## Secondary lower-body skill track — lateral/rotational mobility + strength
1. Supported lateral squat / shallow Cossack
2. Controlled full Cossack squat
3. Deeper or lightly loaded Cossack variations
4. Optional advanced branch: cross-behind/rotational squat patterns
5. Optional dragon-squat progression if desired later

## Hinge/balance track
1. Supported bodyweight single-leg RDL
2. Unsupported single-leg RDL
3. Slow-tempo / longer-reach single-leg RDL
4. Loaded backpack/vest single-leg RDL

### Graduation rule
Progress a movement when you can complete the prescribed sets at the top of the current range with:
- clean technique and controlled range;
- roughly **1-2 RIR** for strength movements;
- no sharp pain or joint pinching;
- stable performance across at least **2 sessions**.

Do not force a harder named skill just because it is next on a list. Pick the branch that fits your mobility, control, equipment, and goals.

# Mobility/flexibility coverage

The plan deliberately trains the major areas relevant to running and calisthenics:

- **Ankles:** knee-to-wall, ankle rocks, calf stretching
- **Hip flexors:** couch/hip-flexor stretch
- **Hip rotation:** 90/90 work
- **Adductors/deep squat:** frog stretch, Cossack progression, deep squat holds
- **Hamstrings:** sliders/RDLs + hamstring floss
- **Thoracic spine:** rotations, thread-the-needle
- **Shoulders/scapulae:** wall slides, CARs, band pass-throughs, scapular work
- **Wrists:** wrist prep and stretches for handstand/L-sit work

### Minimum mobility rule
- Normal training day: **~10-15 min** of mobility is built in.
- Busy/low-energy day: do a **2-5 min minimum** rather than making mobility all-or-nothing.
- If desired, add one **25-40 min** easy mobility/yoga session on a recovery day, but not at the expense of the full rest day when recovery is poor.

# Progression and recovery rules

- Normal training: **1-2 RIR** on most strength sets.
- Restart weeks: **2-3 RIR** and/or remove one round from a main pair.
- When all sets hit the top of the range cleanly, progress with one variable:
  - harder variation;
  - +1-2 reps;
  - +5-10 sec hold;
  - less band assistance;
  - slightly more range of motion;
  - or small external load (vest/backpack).
- Do **not** require momentary failure for progression.
- Deload after ~4-6 hard weeks **or sooner if recovery clearly deteriorates**: reduce total working sets by ~30-40% for about one week.
- After 2+ nights of poor sleep or unusual soreness, reduce volume ~20-30% while keeping technique clean.
- Persistent or sharp joint pain is a reason to stop/regress the movement, not push through it.

# Weekly self-audit

The plan is working if most of these are true:
- You are completing the schedule consistently enough to build momentum.
- Push-up, pull-up, dip, lower-body, handstand/L-sit, or other skill levels are stable or improving.
- Just Run sessions are progressing without accumulating excessive leg/joint soreness.
- Mobility benchmarks (deep squat comfort, knee-to-wall, 90/90 control, wall-slide position) are stable or improving.
- No persistent joint pain.
- Sleep/energy are sufficient to recover.

If 2+ items are poor for ~2 weeks, reduce training volume before adding more work.

After completing Zero-to-5K, reassess whether to keep 3 runs/week or move to 2 runs + an optional Zone-2/recovery session based on the next goal.
